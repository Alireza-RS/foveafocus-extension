How FoveaFocus works

    One word at a time

    When a line of print is read, the eyes do not glide along it. They jump from one group of letters to the next and rest there for a moment. Finding the next place to rest, and the start of the next line, is part of the work.

    A reader of this kind takes that part away. Each word is shown in the same place, so the eyes stay where they are. One letter of the word is marked, a little left of its middle, and it always stands at the same spot. The eye rests on it, and takes in the word around it.

    The speed is counted in words a minute. The first words come more slowly, so that the eyes can settle. A full stop, a comma and a long word are each given a little longer, much as a voice would give them.

    Nothing has to be learned first. Start the reading, and look at the marked letter.
